Methodology

Valuation Methods

Earnings-Book Value
√(22.5 × EPS × BVPS)
Maximum price consistent with P/E ≤ 15 and P/B ≤ 1.5 simultaneously
Net Asset Value
(Current Assets − Total Liab.) / Shares × ⅔
Liquidation floor — strong buy signal when price trades below working capital
Normalized Earnings Power
Avg EPS × (8.5 + 2g)
Value based on multi-year average EPS to smooth cyclical earnings volatility
Growth-Adjusted Earnings
(EPS × (8.5 + 2g) × 4.4) / Y
Adjusts for expected earnings growth and prevailing corporate bond yields
Discounted Cash Flow
Σ FCFₜ / (1+r)ₜ + TV
Two-stage free cash flow model with capped growth and conservative terminal value
Dividend Discount
D₁ / (r − g)
Gordon growth model for established dividend payers

Framework Lenses

The Composite Score blends five lenses: Value (30%) — margin of safety from the intrinsic value methods above; Quality (25%) — margins, returns on equity, and cash conversion; Growth (20%) — revenue/EPS/FCF compounding and the PEG ratio; Momentum (15%) — trend versus moving averages, RSI, and trailing returns; Dividend (10%) — yield, payout sustainability, and track record (weight redistributed for non-payers). A severely negative margin of safety caps the overall recommendation at HOLD regardless of the other lenses.

Margin of Safety = (Intrinsic Value − Market Price) / Intrinsic Value. A margin of ≥ 30% indicates sufficient buffer against estimation error for value investing. A margin of ≥ 50% represents a deep value situation. The Financial Health Scorecard evaluates size, liquidity, leverage, earnings consistency, dividend record, and valuation ratios.
